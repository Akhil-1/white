# How the code works (for the group, so you can explain it in class)

`app.py` is about 1,300 lines, in 12 numbered sections. Read it top to bottom once.

## The big idea
Streamlit **reruns the whole file from top to bottom every time the user clicks or types**.
So the file always: (1) styles the page, (2) reads the current inputs, (3) recalculates everything,
(4) draws only the current step.

## Section by section

| # | Section | What to say in class |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Look and feel | `.streamlit/config.toml` sets the light theme (off-white page, deep-navy accent) so every native widget matches. One block of CSS in `app.py` then styles cards (white, hairline, soft shadow), the top bar, pills, option cards, the gold-ruled AI card and restyles Streamlit widgets (buttons, inputs, tabs, segmented controls). A `@media (max-width:760px)` block makes the phone layout: single column, 2×2 KPI tiles, step dots instead of labels, a sticky Back/Next bar. Small SVG icons and the ring/gauge are drawn in code (`icon()`, `ring()`, `gauge()`, `bar3()`). |
| 2 | Helpers | `inr()` formats ₹ in Indian style; `months_to_goal()` simulates saving month by month with growth until the target is reached; `path()` gives the month-by-month balance for the charts. |
| 3 | Session state | `st.session_state` holds the inputs while the user moves between steps. Sample numbers are pre-filled for the demo. Several goals are kept in `S.goals`; `load_goal()`, `add_goal()` and `remove_goal()` swap the active goal (`S.gi`) in and out of the six goal fields (`GOAL_KEYS`), and `total_goal_monthly` adds up what all goals need each month. `go()` and `nav_changed()` keep the step buttons and the step tabs in sync. |
| 3b | History in the browser | A 12-line `st.components.v2` component (`STORE_JS`) reads and writes one key in the browser's `localStorage`. "Save this month's check-in" (step 4) stores the month's summary plus the inputs; the welcome screen then shows past check-ins, "Use last month's numbers" reloads them, and step 1 shows the change vs last month. Nothing ever reaches a server; clearing the browser clears the history. Text coming back from the store is HTML-escaped (`esc()`) before display. |
| 4 | The AI capability | `explain()` sends a **task** plus the **already-calculated facts** to Gemini with a fixed system prompt (use only given numbers, no fund names, no "best option", ≤80 words). If there is no key or the call fails, it shows a rule-based sentence instead, so the app never breaks. "What's sent to the AI" shows the exact JSON. `call_chat()`/`ask()` power "Ask about your plan" in step 4: a second prompt (`CHAT_PROMPT`) that may answer only from the plan's facts, so it is part of the plan screen, not a standalone chatbot. |
| 5 | Calculations | Fixed rules: free money = salary − commitments − usual spend; buffer = savings ÷ monthly living costs; needed per month = (goal − saved) ÷ months left; `monthly_needed()` = the same with growth (closed form: how much per month each mix needs to be on time); risk comfort = score from 3 answers; each mix's expected return = weighted average of editable assumptions; likely range uses equity at 4% and 15%. `mix_stats()` adds, per mix: value by the target date (`fv`), months early or late (`late`), the rupee fall in a bad year (`drop_amt`) and a risk index (Steady 2, Balanced 3, Growth 4 dots). FD and loan interest are deliberately not projected. |
| 6 | Charts | Altair, light theme. `goal_chart()` = savings lines with a dashed goal line, target-date line and hover tooltip (step 2). `range_chart()` = when each mix could reach the goal: dot = expected (with its date written next to it), bar = likely range, dashed line = your target (step 3). `value_chart()` = money each mix would hold by the target date against a dashed goal line (step 3). The salary split (step 1) and the debt/equity/gold split are simple stacked bars in HTML (`bar3()`); the emergency buffer is an SVG gauge (0–6 months, 3-month marker) and goal progress an SVG ring. Chart colours (Steady blue, Balanced orange, Growth green) were checked for colour-blind safety on white. |
| 7 | Top bar and step tabs | Logo ("White." with the navy full stop), AI on/off toggle, and the step row: a `st.segmented_control` whose labels are numbered with `format_func`; `nav_changed()` keeps it in sync with the Back/Next buttons. On phones the top bar also shows four progress dots (`progress_html()`). |
| 8–12 | The five screens | Welcome → Where I stand (tabbed inputs; hero "free this month" with salary bar, 4 KPI tiles, buffer gauge) → Set a goal (goal-type pills, goal card with progress ring and On track / Behind pill, pace chart) → Investment options (3 risk questions as segmented choices, goal-date range chart, 3 option cards each with a "Choose" button that sets `S.choice` through `pick()`) → My plan (plan card, "Where the goal money sits", a "You miss … by N months" card with four ways back on track, "What if…?" with a before → after timeline, "Ask about your plan", download). The welcome screen is a salary-day tracker: one card per goal (ring, a bar for where today's salary takes it, an inline SVG sparkline of your pace vs the on-time line, date at this pace, needed per month). `mark_paid()` handles "I've put aside": it adds the monthly amount to that goal, records the month in `S.paid`, and saves the check-in to browser storage; reopening the app the same month picks it back up (`load_last(nav=False)`). Home also has a quick "Thinking of buying something?" check (`quick_amt`, `quick_src`): from free money it shows free money left; from goal money it shows the delay in days or months at the current pace. `runway()` builds the salary-to-salary balance day by day from the due days (`rent_day`, `emi_day`, `sip_day`, `bills_day`) and `runway_chart()` draws it as a step line; it ends at the same "left free" figure as My plan. `simulate()` runs one mix month by month on a scenario's market path (the same path for every mix, seeded so it repeats); `sim_section()` draws the chart, the comparison table and the check-in cards. `assistant()` draws the pinned "Ask White" popover; `rule_answer()` reads the question's intent and amount and answers from the person's numbers, and Gemini, when on, rephrases that calculated answer. `money()` pairs a slider (`key__s`) with a number box (`key`); `_from_slider` and `_from_box` keep them in sync, and the app always reads the box's key. The navy header is `st.container(key="masthead")`, stretched to full width with CSS. Risk questions sit question-left / choice-right on a laptop. Desktop: inputs left, results right. |
| 12 | "What if…?" check (in step 4) | The user enters a one-off spend or a change to the monthly amount. The same `months_to_goal()` rule recalculates the goal date and the buffer before and after; Gemini explains the trade-off without saying whether to do it. |

## Why the AI is designed this way (link to your research)
- **Rules calculate, AI explains**: numbers must be correct and repeatable; language models can make arithmetic mistakes.
- **AI never picks an option**: research respondents wanted to keep the final decision.
- **Only summary numbers are sent**: privacy concerns from interviews and the Reddit thread.
- **Works without AI**: graceful fallback, and the user can turn AI off.
- **History stays in the browser**: month-over-month comparison without an account, a database or a server copy of anyone's money.

## Things to try changing yourselves (good for "debug and iterate")
- Change the three mixes in `MIXES` (section 5).
- Edit the rules in `SYSTEM_PROMPT` (section 4) and see how the explanation changes.
- Change sample numbers in `DEFAULTS` (section 3) to match your persona.
- Change the colours in `:root` (section 1) or in `.streamlit/config.toml`; the brand accent is `#14213D`, gold `#B8862B` is reserved for the AI card.
- If Gemini says the model is not found, set `GEMINI_MODEL = "gemini-2.5-flash"` (or a current model name from aistudio.google.com) in secrets.
